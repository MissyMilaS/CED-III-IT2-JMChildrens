Sentiment Analysis on Reddit Comments (GoEmotions): Dense, RNN, LSTM and BiLSTM Models
Sarah Rodriguez  |  Maria Camila Salamanca  |  Nicholas Solís  |  Santiago Cabal  |  Heidy Hernandez
Computing and Discrete Structures III (CyED III) — Integrative Task 2, 2026-2  |  Universidad Icesi
Abstract—This paper investigates binary sentiment classification on informal, noisy, and sarcastic Reddit comments sampled from the GoEmotions dataset (Demszky et al., 2020). We implement and systematically benchmark a stratified Dummy baseline alongside four neural architectures: a Feedforward Dense network with averaged embeddings, a vanilla RNN, a Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) network, and an additional Bidirectional LSTM (BiLSTM), each tuned via 3-fold stratified cross-validation and evaluated across five distinct random seeds on a balanced test set of 200 comments. The BiLSTM achieved the superior and most balanced classification performance (accuracy 0.677 ± 0.039, macro F1 0.674 ± 0.019, Cohen's κ 0.354 ± 0.078), effectively resolving the severe positive-class recall degradation observed in both the Dense model (recall 0.496) and the unidirectional LSTM (recall 0.422). Crucially, the vanilla RNN proved unstable due to vanishing gradients (accuracy 0.558 ± 0.032, κ 0.116 ± 0.064), barely surpassing random chance. We interpret these findings through foundational theoretical models of computation, drawing direct parallels between recurrent gated memory mechanisms and Turing Machine tape manipulation primitives.

1. Introduction and Objectives

Sentiment analysis of short user-generated texts from social platforms such as Reddit represents a foundational challenge in modern Natural Language Processing (NLP). Unlike edited formal corpora, Reddit comments are characterized by heavy colloquialisms, non-standard punctuation, syntactic noise, implicit emotional cues, and irony. In this regime, sequence processing models capable of maintaining context across token positions are essential.

From a theoretical computer science perspective, sequence processing models embody classical formal automata and computability concepts. While standard feedforward architectures process tokens as order-invariant collections, recurrent neural networks maintain an evolving internal state—acting as a finite-precision bounded memory over an input tape, conceptually linked to the state transitions and read/write operations of Turing Machines.

General Objective: Build, tune, evaluate, and comparatively analyze supervised sentiment classifiers on a strictly balanced subset of GoEmotions (500 positive and 500 negative comments).

Specific Objectives:

  • Curate and preprocess the multi-label GoEmotions corpus into a rigorously balanced binary benchmark, performing exploratory data analysis (EDA) across class distributions and token lengths.
  • Establish a baseline (DummyClassifier) and develop three primary neural architectures: Dense Feedforward NN, vanilla RNN, and LSTM, performing hyperparameter optimization for each.
  • Implement an additional Bidirectional LSTM (BiLSTM) model to assess whether bidirectional sequential reading enhances sentiment resolution compared to unidirectional recurrence.
  • Evaluate all models across standard performance metrics (Accuracy, Precision, Recall, F1-score, Cohen's κ) and establish formal connections to bounded memory and Turing Machine computability.

2. Dataset, Sentiment Labeling and Preprocessing

2.1 Dataset and Sentiment Identification

We utilized the Reddit GoEmotions corpus (Demszky et al., ACL 2020), composed of 54,263 comments labeled across 27 fine-grained emotions plus neutral by human raters with an agreement threshold of ≥2 raters. Following the official taxonomy, emotions are mapped into three meta-categories: Positive (12 emotions, e.g., amusement, joy, love, gratitude), Negative (11 emotions, e.g., anger, sadness, disgust, annoyance), and Ambiguous (4 emotions, e.g., surprise, curiosity).

To construct an unambiguous benchmark, comments were filtered according to strict criteria: comments with ambiguous, neutral, or conflicting multi-group labels were discarded, retaining exclusively single-group positive or negative texts. Identical text strings with conflicting sentiment labels were purged, text duplicates were eliminated, and exactly 500 positive and 500 negative comments were sampled using fixed seed SEED = 42.

2.2 Text Preprocessing and Vectorization

Text preprocessing was implemented in src/preprocessing.py with explicit design rationale:

  • Lowercasing & Typographic Normalization: Converts all characters to lowercase; standardizes smart quotes (’) to plain apostrophes so contractions (e.g., didn't, it's) remain cohesive tokens.
  • URL & Noise Removal: Web URLs are stripped via regular expressions as they contain no intrinsic sentiment.
  • Entity & Religion Markers: GoEmotions anonymization tokens [NAME] and [RELIGION] are explicitly preserved by regex tokenization (TOKEN_RE) rather than being stripped as punctuation. Emojis and standard punctuation symbols are discarded.
  • Selective Stopword Filtration: English stopwords from NLTK were removed, with a crucial exception: negation tokens (not, no, never, nor, cannot, n't) were strictly preserved, as their deletion fundamentally inverts sentiment polarity (e.g., "not good" vs. "good").

Vectorization for neural architectures was built strictly on training partitions to prevent data leakage. Tokens are mapped into integer sequences bounded by a maximum sequence length of MAX_LEN = 30 (covering >99% of sample token lengths). Shorter sequences are right-padded with <pad> (index 0), while out-of-vocabulary tokens map to <unk> (index 1). The vocabulary constructed exclusively on train contains 2,215 unique tokens.

The 1,000-comment balanced dataset was partitioned into an 80/20 train/test split (800 train, 200 test) using stratified sampling (random_state=42), preserving an exact 50/50 class balance in both subsets. Hyperparameter search employed 3-fold stratified cross-validation on train only.

2.3 Exploratory Data Analysis

Exploratory analysis confirmed that the corpus possesses zero null cells, zero duplicated text instances, and zero empty strings following text cleaning. As displayed in Figure 2, raw comments averaged 13.18 words. Cleaned token sequences average 7.16 tokens (maximum 21 tokens). Crucially, sentence length distributions are virtually indistinguishable between positive and negative classes (median length of 7 tokens for both), proving that comment length provides zero discriminatory power, forcing models to rely strictly on semantic and structural token representations.

3. Models and Training Procedure

3.1 Baseline Model

We implemented sklearn.dummy.DummyClassifier evaluated under four strategies (stratified, most_frequent, prior, uniform; seed 42). Under most_frequent, the classifier degenerates to predicting exclusively the majority class, achieving 50.0% accuracy but 0.0 recall, F1, and κ. The stratified strategy reproduces random guessing aligned with prior distributions, yielding 50.0% accuracy, F1 = 0.505, and κ = 0.000, establishing the rigorous empirical performance floor.

3.2 Dense Neural Network

The Dense architecture represents each comment by computing the mean of its learned word embeddings across non-padded token positions: h = (1/L)∑t=1L E[wt]. The sentence representation h ∈ ℝ64 passes through a Linear layer with ReLU activation (32 units), a Dropout regularization layer (rate 0.5), and a final Linear projection to a single scalar logit, trained with binary cross-entropy with logits.

3.3 Vanilla Recurrent Neural Network (SimpleRNN)

The vanilla RNN sequentially ingests token embeddings xt ∈ ℝ64, updating an internal hidden state: ht = tanh(Wih xt + bih + Whh ht-1 + bhh). The final hidden state hL is passed through Dropout (0.5) and mapped via a linear projection to the output logit. Standard backpropagation through time (BPTT) in vanilla RNNs suffers from exponential gradient decay (∂ht/∂hk → 0 as t − k increases), severely constraining its ability to retain sentiment context.

3.4 Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM)

The LSTM overcomes vanishing gradients by introducing an explicit additive cell state Ct regulated by three non-linear multiplicative gating mechanisms: forget gate ft = σ(Wf xt + Uf ht-1 + bf), input gate it = σ(Wi xt + Ui ht-1 + bi), candidate memory C̃t = tanh(Wc xt + Uc ht-1 + bc), cell state update Ct = ft ⊙ Ct-1 + it ⊙ C̃t, output gate ot = σ(Wo xt + Uo ht-1 + bo), and hidden state ht = ot ⊙ tanh(Ct). The linear gradient highway through Ct prevents vanishing gradients across token spans.

3.5 Bidirectional LSTM (BiLSTM — Additional Model)

The BiLSTM executes two independent LSTM recurrent passes over each comment sequence: a forward pass (left-to-right, yielding h⃗L) and a backward pass (right-to-left, yielding h⃖1). Both terminal representations are concatenated: hBi = [ h⃗L ; h⃖1 ] ∈ ℝ64. This enables tokens to integrate context simultaneously from both preceding and succeeding words. The computational penalty entails doubling recurrent weights (25,153 parameters above embeddings vs. 12,577 in LSTM) and training runtime (6.3s vs. 2.9s per run).

3.6 Hyperparameter Tuning and Training Protocol

Hyperparameter tuning was executed over an exhaustive 18-configuration grid search per model: hidden units ∈ {32, 64, 128}, batch size ∈ {16, 32, 64}, learning rate ∈ {0.001, 0.003}, utilizing Adam optimizer, gradient clipping at max norm 1.0, and 3-fold stratified cross-validation on train. Fixed hyperparameters included embedding dimension 64 and dropout 0.5. Early stopping monitored validation loss with patience 5 (maximum 30 epochs), restoring optimal model weights.

4.1 Qualitative Error Analysis

To diagnose failure modes, we examined representative test comments exhibiting misclassification across models:

  • Sarcasm & Irony: "Just goes to show it takes 0 skill to play that hero" (Ground Truth: Positive / amusement, ID: ef1q5yu). Predicted as Negative by LSTM and Dense due to the co-occurrence of "0 skill" in gaming vernacular. Lexical sentiment classifiers fail when surface tokens contradict pragmatic intent.
  • Negated Emotional Expressions: "It’s not significantly more unpleasant than the flu vaccine." (Ground Truth: Negative, ID: eczvoxz). Dense misclassified this as Positive because averaging embeddings diluted the scoped negation "not" against the multi-token medical description. BiLSTM correctly classified it negative by capturing left-to-right and right-to-left syntactic dependency.
  • Understated Distress: "I sleep alone on the floor. That was a bad idea..." (Ground Truth: Negative, ID: ecziz4m). Correctly resolved by BiLSTM, while Vanilla RNN predicted positive due to state corruption across the trailing ellipsis.

5. Comparative Analysis

5.1 Performance Hierarchy and Variance

The BiLSTM demonstrated the highest overall performance, achieving 0.677 accuracy, 0.674 F1, and 0.354 Cohen's κ. It surpassed the stratified baseline by +17.7 accuracy points, exceeding the 95% confidence interval (±6.5 points on N=200). Dense followed at 0.648 accuracy and κ = 0.296 (+14.8 over baseline), while unidirectional LSTM attained 0.625 accuracy and κ = 0.250 (+12.5 over baseline). Vanilla RNN failed to establish reliable separation from chance (0.558 accuracy, κ = 0.116; seed 779 collapsed to 0.495 accuracy).

Crucially, while Dense and LSTM achieved high precision (0.723 and 0.720), both suffered from severe positive-class recall degradation (0.496 and 0.422), misclassifying over half of all positive test comments. In contrast, BiLSTM achieved exceptional class symmetry (0.695 precision, 0.670 recall) and exhibited the tightest F1 standard deviation across seeds (±0.019 vs. ±0.082 in Dense and ±0.091 in LSTM).

5.2 Training Complexity and Overfitting

In all architectures, the shared embedding layer (2,215 tokens × 64 = 141,760 parameters) accounted for 85–99% of total model capacity. Recurrent overhead ranged from 2,113 parameters above embeddings in Dense to 25,153 in BiLSTM. On CPU hardware, training runtime scaled from 0.5s per run (Dense) to 6.3s (BiLSTM).

Grid search revealed that increasing hidden capacity beyond 32 units led to immediate validation degradation (BiLSTM CV accuracy dropped from 0.64 with 32 units to 0.57–0.62 with 64 or 128 units). Early stopping routinely triggered between epochs 2 and 4 for recurrent models, indicating that small corpora (800 training comments) impose severe generalization ceilings regardless of parameter scale.

5.3 Ablation: Stopword Retention Impact

Retaining full stopword sequences (text_mode='full') degraded model performance: Vanilla RNN dropped from 0.558 to 0.533 accuracy, and BiLSTM dropped from 0.677 to 0.629 accuracy, while LSTM remained stagnant (0.625 vs. 0.617). In short comments, non-sentiment stopwords dilute the embedding density without offering grammatical dependencies that small recurrent models can effectively exploit.

5.4 Interpretability and Saliency

Model transparency correlates inversely with recurrence complexity:

  • Dense NN: Highly interpretable. Because input representation is a linear average of token embeddings prior to ReLU activation, individual token contributions can be directly inspected via cosine similarity and vector attribution.
  • Vanilla RNN & LSTM: Decisions are mediated through an evolving, non-linear hidden vector ht. In LSTM, gate activations (input/forget/output) offer partial interpretability regarding information preservation, but lack isolated token attribution.
  • BiLSTM: Least directly interpretable. Concatenating forward and backward hidden states entangles token influence across past and future contexts, requiring gradient saliency backpropagation for explanation.

While infinite-precision RNNs are theoretically Turing-complete (Siegelmann & Sontag, 1995), real-world fixed-precision implementations operate as finite-state automata with bounded memory. The LSTM forget and input gates physically mimic write and erase operations on a bounded tape cell, directly explaining why LSTM outperforms the memoryless overwrite dynamics of vanilla RNNs. However, because sequence lengths are bounded (L ≤ 30), all inputs belong to a finite language where decidability is guaranteed and halting is trivially enforced by sequence termination.

5.6 Optimal Model Selection

Selection: Bidirectional LSTM (BiLSTM). Although the Dense model delivers extreme training velocity (0.5s vs 6.3s) and competitive accuracy (0.648), its catastrophic recall imbalance (0.496) makes it inadequate for balanced sentiment detection. The BiLSTM achieves the highest overall accuracy (0.677), balanced recall/precision (0.670 / 0.695), and the lowest variance across random seeds (±0.019 F1), justifying the modest recurrent compute overhead.

6. Conclusions and Future Work

This investigation evaluated supervised sentiment classification on 1,000 GoEmotions Reddit comments. Our experiments demonstrate that bidirectional sequence processing (BiLSTM) significantly stabilizes classification and resolves false-negative bias on informal text, achieving 0.677 accuracy and 0.354 κ. Vanilla RNNs are severely handicapped by gradient decay, failing to outperform random baselines reliably. The primary constraint on absolute accuracy remains the restricted training sample size (800 comments) and the prevalence of irony and slang.

Future Work: Expanding training to the complete 54,263 GoEmotions corpus; incorporating pretrained embeddings (GloVe, FastText) or contextual Transformer models (BERT, DistilBERT) with self-attention; incorporating emoji embeddings; and extending classification to multi-label emotion prediction.
